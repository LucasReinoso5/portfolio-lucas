Green Drop

An academic business concept for making post-fire reforestation more accessible through biodegradable seed spheres and flexible distribution methods.

Context
ENIT — Six-person team project
Year
2025
Primary role
Market analysis & marketing strategy

01 — The challenge

Restore land that is difficult to reach

Areas affected by wildfires can be dangerous, extensive, and difficult to access. Green Drop explored a simple and scalable way to support reforestation while adapting seed selection and intervention strategies to local ecosystems.

The project was developed as an academic entrepreneurship and business-planning exercise rather than as a commercially launched company.

02 — The concept

Product, distribution, and service

The proposed solution combined biodegradable four-centimeter spheres containing local seeds, nutrients, and a water-retention agent with adapted launchers or drone-based dispersal for hard-to-reach terrain.

A consulting service completed the offer by addressing species selection, intervention density, priority areas, scheduling, and follow-up recommendations.

03 — My role

Understand the market and shape the offer

As part of a six-person team, I contributed to the overall development and evaluation of the project while taking primary responsibility for market analysis and marketing strategy.

  • Segmented the market across public authorities, emergency services, forestry organizations, environmental professionals, associations, and private landowners.
  • Analyzed the competitive environment and positioned Green Drop around accessibility, scalability, and local ecological adaptation.
  • Defined pricing approaches for professional volumes, community kits, launchers, and consulting services.
  • Designed a direct B2B distribution strategy and identified longer-term retail and forestry partnerships.
  • Planned communication through demonstrations, institutional outreach, local events, digital channels, and regional media.

04 — Business model

Evaluate the complete operating system

The team examined how the concept could operate beyond its value proposition. The work covered sourcing, manufacturing steps, quality control, packaging, direct distribution, human resources, industrial-property strategy, and legal structure.

Financial planning included initial investment needs, working-capital requirements, projected statements, loan repayment, contribution margin, and break-even analysis.

06 — What I learned

Connect a technical idea to a viable market

Green Drop taught me to evaluate an engineering concept as a complete business system. A technically interesting idea also needs identifiable customers, a clear value proposition, workable operations, coherent pricing, and a realistic route to market.

The project strengthened my ability to collaborate across marketing, operations, finance, legal, and organizational decisions while remaining accountable for a defined area of ownership.
